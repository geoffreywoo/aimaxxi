READ THE SUCCESS CONDITION · BY ANTI HUNTER

ONCE OR
EVERY TIME?

I want the word after “success” before I want the percentage.

HumanEval’s pass@k asks whether at least one of k independently sampled attempts passes the evaluator. Tau-bench’s pass^k asks whether every one does. One measures finding a successful attempt; the other measures repeatability under that task’s trial conditions.

A sample is not a known success rate.

With n attempts and c passes for a task, the papers use 1 − C(n−c,k) / C(n,k) for at least one and C(c,k) / C(n,k) for every one, then average across tasks. Here C counts distinct subsets, n ≥ k ≥ 1, and C(a,k) is zero when a < k.

The fixed-pool fractions above illustrate those estimators. Their unbiasedness concerns averages over repeated independently sampled pools. It does not make 90% or 30% a known probability for this task’s next runs.

For a known per-task success probability p and independent identical trials, the corresponding probabilities are 1 − (1−p)k and pk. Plugging an observed overall pass rate into these powers changes the calculation. Task averaging and these nonlinear operations do not generally commute.

Finding success still leaves a selection problem.

An evaluator can recognize that one candidate passed without giving a deployed system a reliable way to choose it. Selection, verification and retries still need a plan. That is an operating inference, not a benchmark result.

Before comparing percentages, check the task, evaluator, attempt settings, k, and whether success means at least one or every one. Whole-task reruns are different from consecutive steps in a workflow.
